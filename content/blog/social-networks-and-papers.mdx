# Paper 1: Model-Specific Echo Chamber Formation Patterns in Repeated LLM Agent Networks 
[Link to paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3816713.3816738)

## The Gaps
- Existing papers explore how to improve collaboration in multi-agentic systems,
or focus on usefulness of diversity. 
They don't examine the results that different models will produce in the same 
experimental considerations
- Models may be prone to sycophancy, reducing over all reliability of the results 
- Models may converge to an opinion, this does not mean that the consensus is the best result
- Some research suggests that even smth as small as how the models come to decisions,
can influence what the final consensus of a discussion be

## Their Work
this paper examines:
- how echo chambers form across different models 
-whether the trajectory changes with the type of model being used

To study this, they had model-specific groups repeatedly interact while the topics,
network struc, recommendation rules, were all kept the same.

they compared the values of - 
1. Polarization Index (Pz): Measures the extent to which a network's population is divided into distinct groups with opposing beliefs. 
2. Global Disagreement (DG): Measures the overall difference in opinions between all pairs of individuals in a network, regardless of whether they are directly connected.
3. Neighbor Correlation Index (NCI): Measures how strongly individuals' beliefs correlate with the beliefs of their immediate network neighbors (people they are connected to).
4. Mean Belief Shift: Measures the average change in individuals' beliefs over a given period or number of interaction rounds.

To further check if their results were due to the interaction style or repeated updating,
an auxillary no interaction control study was done for GPT-4o

### Experiment Design
- Independent variable: type of model used to make the agent group 
- Dependent variables: Pz, DG, NCI, and Mean Belief Shift
- Models used: GPT-4o,GPT-5.4-mini, Llama-3.3-70B, Mistral-Small-24B, Gemma-3n-E4B, and GLM-4.5-Air. 
- Topic chosen for models to debate: Is there such a thing as a paradox of free speech?

1 experiment = 1 group 
num of agents in 1 group = 50
num of rounds = 30
network struc remains same for all experiments
recommendation algorithm remains same for all experiments

note - 
experimental scale and the network-based interaction struc-ture were designed by referring to the LLM-based social opinionsimulation framework of Wang et al. [1].

- The agent network used a fixed Watts–Strogatz small-world topology with 50 agents, andthe same network setting was applied to all model groups. 
More specifically, the network was initialized with k = 6 and rewiring probability p = 0.1, and this topology was kept the same within each run
- for each round, each neighbour received 5 opinion texts from neighbours who were selected as per the
rules of the recommendation system (it picked agents whos belief difference was less than 2.0 with the current agent)
- opinion update step: each agent receives a structured prompt that includes its persona, 
current belief, current opinion text, long-term memory, and the opinion texts of the recommended neighbors.
- an interaction condition and a no-interaction condition for the GPT-4o group was also studied
- In the no-interaction setting, the neighbouring opinion texts were not provided to the agent
The agent used its own persona and configuration to reflect on its opinion. The update
step was similar to the interaction setting.  

### Persona
The attributes were:
- age
- gender
- big 5 personality trait
- opinion value (range of [-2, 2])
- short term memory
- long term memory


## Results
With respect to convergence or diagreement:

- GPT-4o: fastest convergence pattern
- GLM-4.5-Air: flat pattern with no clear change
- llama-3.3-70B, Mistral-Small-24B, and GPT-5.4-mini: more repeated interactions 
- lead to more dispersion and disagreements

With respect to Pz:
- GPT-4o: Pz decreased rapidly from 1.12 to 0.33 in round 10 and 0.07 in round 30
This meant that the difference in opinion between diff groups greatly reduced, the more they interacted
- Mistral-Small-24B, GPT-5.4-mini, Gemma-3n-E4B: increase in Pz through more interactions. 
Rapid increase for Mistral-Small-24B,  GPT-5.4-mini and moderate increase for Gemma-3n-E4B
- GLM-4.5-Air: Not much change in Pz at all 

With respect to DG:
- GPT-4o: DG increased quickly i.e. 
difference of opinion among neighbouring agents reduced rapidly
- Mistral-Small-24B, GPT-5.4-mini, Gemma-3n-E4B: DG decreased i.e. larger difference in opinion with neighbouring agents
-  GLM-4.5-Air: Not much change in DG at all 

## Main finding
the type of model affects the final outcome of a discourse in agentic networks, even if the
network structure, recommendation algo, topics, language, etc. are the same.


# Paper 2: Multilingual Agent-Based World Modeling for Social Science
[Link to paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.07195)

## Motivation
- opinions and attitudes on global issues are formed through multilingual and
multimodal online discourse
- if agents can take human memory and emulate human behavior, they can act as proxies
for humans in social scientific research or complement them 
- Agent-based modeling (ABM) is already using in applications like:
1. studying group/societal behaviour
2. interpreting social phenomena
3. exploring social theories
4. predicting policy outcomes

## The Gaps
- cross-lingual and cross-cultural dynamics are understudied in ABMs
- 

## Their work
- Create  Multilingual Agent-based World Modeling (MAWM) framework, for societal 
modelling in a multi-turn format between LLM agents with diverse personas
- Construction of Multilingual Agent Perspective Survey (MAPS), a benchmark dataset 
with  survey questions from GlobalOpinionQA, demographic personas derived from the World Values Survey,
spanning 50 countries and 28 languages across geographically diverse regions.
- experiments and case studies on cultural assimilation and normative diffusion

### Dataset
-  they pair user settings sampled from multicultural populations with a broad set of social research 
questions
- for constructing user settings, they draw personas from World Values Survey
- Persona attributes: age, education, gender, marital status, occupation, political preference,
religion, and social class
- For multilingual agent simulation, a native language is assigned
based on country

### Framework
MAWM is a framework where multi-turn interaction takes place between users and new organisation
agents. 
news org agents will generate posts to influence users 
users will compose posts, read recommended posts, and can vote on survey questions

# Paper 3: Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes
[Link to paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03407)

## Overview
Models may employ strategies like obscuring their CoT to hide traces of deception, rendering a monitor agent useless. This paper evaluates
whether linear probes can robustly identify deception or not.

## Their work

### Datasets used
they use 2 datasets, 
- one w contrasting instructions to be honest or deceptive 
- one w responses to simple roleplaying scenarios

aim is to test whether linear probes generalise to actual settings of deceptive behaviour by Llama-3.3-70BInstruct.
this paper focuses on <b>strategic deception<\b> ie the model focuses on inducing false beliefs to achieve some goal.

### Motivation
Big alignment risk is AI pursuing misaligned goals while also deceiving and hiding it's motives and actions.
For e.g. sandbagging their evaluations or falsely pretending as if they are aligned

### gaps 
- prev work does not consider multi-turn conversations so their approach may not generalize well to these settings
- evaluates human written responses, not agent written
- focus on factual domains
